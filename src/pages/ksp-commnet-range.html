How CommNet range works

Every antenna has a power rating, a plain number such as 500k for a Communotron 16 or 100G for a Communotron 88-88. Two points can talk when the distance between them is less than the geometric mean of their ratings:

Maximum range = √(power A × power B)

The Deep Space Network counts as one end of the link, with a power of 2G, 50G or 250G depending on the level of your Tracking Station. A strong ground station helps a weak probe a lot, which is why upgrading the Tracking Station is often cheaper than adding dishes.

Several antennas on one craft

Craft power = strongest × (sum of all ÷ strongest) ^ average exponent

Antennas stack with diminishing returns. The exponent is an average of each antenna's "combinability exponent", weighted by power. Almost every antenna uses 0.75. The two exceptions are the Communotron 16, at 1 (it stacks in full), and the 16-S, at 0 (extra copies add nothing). The built-in antenna in pods and probe cores doesn't stack at all.

Signal strength

x = 1 − distance ÷ maximum range
Signal = (3 − 2x) × x²

Signal is 100% up close and falls along an S-shaped curve to 0% at maximum range. It is exactly 50% at half the range, and it only reads a full 100% within about 4% of the range. A stronger signal also gets more science out of transmitted experiments, and through relays the strengths multiply hop by hop.

Worked example: a Duna probe

A probe carries two HG-55 dishes (15G each). Combined, it has 15G × (30G ÷ 15G)^0.75 = 25.23G. Talking to a level 3 Tracking Station (250G), its range is √(25.23G × 250G) = 79.4 Gm. Kerbin and Duna are never more than 35.4 Gm apart, so the link always holds. At 30 Gm, x = 1 − 30 ÷ 79.4 = 0.62, giving a signal of 68%.

With a level 2 Tracking Station (50G), the range drops to 35.5 Gm. That barely covers Duna at its farthest, and at 30 Gm the signal is just 6%. On Hard difficulty (range modifier 0.65), the level 3 range falls to 64.0 Gm, still plenty.

Relays multiply: if the probe reaches a relay at 80% and the relay reaches Kerbin at 90%, the signal home is 0.8 × 0.9 = 72%.

What this leaves out

  • Line of sight. Planets and moons block signals. A probe behind Duna has no link whatever its range; that is what relays in high orbits are for.
  • Which antennas a relay uses. A relay passes other craft's signals using only its relay antennas (HG-5, RA-2, RA-15, RA-100), but its own link home uses all its antennas. Tick "It's a relay" when the other craft is relaying for you.
  • Ground stations. With "extra ground stations" turned off, only the KSC has the full DSN rating.
  • Plasma blackout during re-entry, if that difficulty option is on.
  • Making History and Breaking Ground antennas. Only antennas from the base game are listed.

Tips

  • For the Mun and Minmus, a single Communotron 16 reaches a level 2 Tracking Station (158 Mm) easily.
  • Three relays in the same orbit, 120° apart, keep each other in view around any planet and cover almost every point on the surface.
  • Direct antennas (DTS-M1, HG-55, 88-88) can't relay. A relay antenna also works as a direct one, so a relay satellite needs nothing else.
