The level curve

Each level takes more points than the one before, and the step gets steeper twice: at level 16 and level 31.

From levelPoints to the next levelTotal points to reach level L
0 to 152 × level + 7L² + 6L
16 to 305 × level − 382.5L² − 40.5L + 360
31 and up9 × level − 1584.5L² − 162.5L + 2220

The total formulas apply from the start of each band (levels 0-16, 17-31 and 32 up), because the total at level L is the sum of every step below it.

Worked example: level 0 to 30

Level 30 is the most an enchanting table charges for its best offer. The total to reach it is 2.5 × 30² − 40.5 × 30 + 360 = 2,250 − 1,215 + 360 = 1,395 points. A Bottle o' Enchanting drops 3 to 11 points, 7 on average, so that is about 1,395 ÷ 7 ≈ 200 bottles.

Going from 30 to 31 alone takes 5 × 30 − 38 = 112 points, more than levels 0 to 7 put together (7 + 9 + 11 + 13 + 15 + 17 + 19 = 91). That's why it pays to spend levels as you earn them rather than bank them.

Spending levels well

  • Enchanting table: it needs you to have up to 30 levels but only takes 1 to 3. Use it at level 30, then earn the few levels back.
  • Anvil: it takes every level it charges, so each job is cheapest in points when you have just enough levels. The enchantment order calculator keeps each job small.
  • Dying: you drop only 7 points per level, and never more than 100. At level 30 you lose 1,395 points but can pick up only 100 of them, which is enough for level 7.
  • Mending uses experience orbs before they reach your bar, while a Mending item you're holding or wearing is damaged.

What it leaves out

Points from one source vary: bottles drop 3 to 11, and mobs and ores give random amounts. The bottle count is an average, so expect a few more or less. The level curve itself is exact and is the same in Java and Bedrock.

Sources: experience formulas, the Bottle o' Enchanting range and the death drop as given in the Minecraft Wiki "Experience" article, checked 21 November 2026 (Java Edition 26.3).
