KSP transfer window calculator

Game data: KSP 1.12.5 (stock)

When to leave, where in your orbit to burn, and how much delta-v the burn needs, for any two planets around Kerbol or any two moons of the same planet.

How it works

The cheapest way between two circular orbits around the same body is a Hohmann transfer: half an ellipse that just touches both orbits. You leave from one side of it and arrive on the far side, so the target has to be in the right place when you set off. That is the phase angle: how far ahead of you (positive) or behind you (negative) the target must be, measured around the parent body.

Transfer time t = π × √(a³ ÷ μ), where a = (r₁ + r₂) ÷ 2
Phase angle = 180° − (target's angular speed × t)
Ejection burn = √(v∞² + 2μ ÷ r) − √(μ ÷ r)

Here r₁ and r₂ are the two orbits' radii, μ is the parent body's gravitational parameter, and v∞ is the extra speed you need once you have escaped your starting planet. You don't burn v∞ directly: you burn from a low parking orbit, where the planet's own gravity well gives you some of it for free (the Oberth effect). That is why the burn from a 100 km Kerbin orbit is smaller than you might guess.

The ejection angle says where in your parking orbit to burn so that you leave the planet's sphere of influence heading the right way. For a target farther from the parent (Duna, Jool), you need to exit along your planet's direction of travel, so you burn on the side facing away from it. For a target closer in (Eve, Moho), you exit backwards and burn on the other side.

The next window comes from each body's starting position in the game's data (its position at Year 1, Day 1) and its orbital speed. The calculator finds when the angle between them next equals the phase angle.

Worked example: Kerbin to Duna

Kerbin orbits Kerbol at 13.6 Gm, Duna at 20.7 Gm. The transfer ellipse's semi-major axis is their average, 17.2 Gm, so the trip takes half its period: 302 days. In that time Duna moves 135.6° round its orbit, so it must start 180° − 135.6° = 44.36° ahead of Kerbin.

Leaving Kerbin's sphere of influence takes v∞ = 918 m/s. From a 100 km orbit, where you are already moving at 2,246 m/s, the burn is √(918² + 2 × 3.5316×10¹² ÷ 700,000) − 2,246 = 1,060 m/s. The escape path bends 149° from the burn point, so burn when your ship is 149° from Kerbin's prograde direction: 31° after it passes Kerbin's retrograde side. You arrive at Duna about 826 m/s faster than Duna itself, which you'll need to cancel with a capture burn or aerobraking.

At the start of a new game Duna is 135.5° ahead of Kerbin. The angle closes slowly, and the first window comes on Year 1, Day 231. After that, windows repeat every 909 days, the synodic period, so the next is Year 3, Day 288.

What this leaves out

  • Eccentric orbits. The maths treats every orbit as a circle at its average distance. That is close for Kerbin, Eve, Duna and Jool. For Moho, Dres, Eeloo, Gilly, Bop and Pol, whose orbits are noticeably oval, the best window and burn can be off by days and by a hundred m/s or more. Use the result as a starting point and fine-tune with a manoeuvre node.
  • Inclination. Moho (7°), Dres (5°), Eeloo (6.15°) and Minmus (6°) orbit at a tilt. Plan a mid-course plane change at the ascending or descending node, or the encounter will miss.
  • Capture. The arrival speed shown is relative to the target as you enter its sphere of influence. The burn to stop depends on how low you capture.
  • From Kerbin to its own moons. The Mun and Minmus orbit Kerbin, so they don't pair with planets here. For a Mun trip, the usual rule is to burn when the Mun is about a quarter-orbit ahead of you.

Tips

  • Time warp to a few days before the window, then make a manoeuvre node at the ejection angle and nudge it until the map shows an encounter.
  • Look up the date in game from the clock in the top-left corner (in Kerbin time, with 6-hour days). If your game uses 24-hour Earth days, convert the date first.
  • Missed a window? Jool and Eeloo windows come round about once a Kerbin year; Duna's only every 2.1 years.
